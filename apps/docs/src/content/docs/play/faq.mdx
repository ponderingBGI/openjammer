## Is OpenJammer free?

Yes. OpenJammer is open source under the **AGPL-3.0** license — free to use, modify, and
distribute. (If you run a modified version as a public web service, AGPL asks you to share
your source.) The desktop installers are hosted free on GitHub Releases.

## Do I need to download anything?

No — you can [play right in your browser](https://openjammer.app) with nothing to install. It
works offline after the first visit. Download the [desktop app](/openjammer/play/install/)
only when you want the lowest latency or to host your own VST3 / AU / CLAP plugins.

## Browser or desktop app — which should I use?

Start in the **browser**: it's the whole instrument, zero install, honest **~15–25 ms**
latency — great for sketching and jamming. Move to the **desktop app** when you want native
**< 5 ms** MIDI-to-audio or plugin hosting. See
[Browser vs native](/openjammer/play/browser-vs-native/) for the full comparison.

## Why is the browser higher latency than the app?

Browsers **cannot use ASIO drivers** — they go through the OS audio layer (WASAPI on Windows,
Core Audio on macOS, ALSA/JACK on Linux), which adds latency. The native desktop app talks to
a small-buffer audio stream directly, which is how it reaches sub-5 ms. A USB interface
narrows the gap in the browser. Full detail in
[Audio setup & latency](/openjammer/play/audio-and-latency/).

## Do I need a USB audio interface?

No, but it's the single biggest latency improvement, especially in the browser. The built-in
sound card is fine for trying things out; a class-compliant USB interface gets you into the
3–10 ms range. See the [recommended interfaces](/openjammer/play/audio-and-latency/).

## Do I need sample packs?

No. All **171 instruments play out of the box**, each backed by a distinct procedural voice —
zero downloads. You can still bring your own samples, SoundFonts (`.sf2`), and CLAP/VST3
plugins when you want to. See [Sound & instruments](/openjammer/play/sound-and-instruments/).

## Where are my files saved?

Your patches are stored **locally** — in browser storage in the PWA, and on-device in the
native app — and you can export/import a patch as a JSON file to share or back up. Nothing is
uploaded to a server; core functionality needs no external API calls, which is why it works on
a stage with no Wi-Fi.

## It's not making sound — help?

Walk through [Troubleshooting](/openjammer/play/troubleshooting/), or press **Ctrl/Cmd+K** and
let the AI read your logs and [fix it for you](/openjammer/play/troubleshooting-with-the-ai/).
