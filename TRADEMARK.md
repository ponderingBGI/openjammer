# Trademark Policy **(DRAFT — pending steward adoption + trademark counsel)**

> **Status: DRAFT proposal.** Not yet a registered mark or an adopted policy. Because the
> [Plugin Exception](LICENSE-EXCEPTION.md) deliberately permits commercial, non-copyleft plugins, the
> **trademark — not the license — is OpenJammer's durable anti-fork moat.** Register the mark **before
> marketplace launch.** This is not legal advice.

## The marks

"**OpenJammer**", the OpenJammer name, and the OpenJammer logo (the "Marks") are marks of the project
steward. Copyright (the AGPL) and trademark are **separate**: the AGPL lets you copy, modify, and
redistribute the *code*; it does **not** grant any right to use the *Marks*.

## What you may do (no permission needed)

- **Nominative use** — truthfully describe compatibility or purpose: "a plugin **for** OpenJammer",
  "**compatible with** OpenJammer", "built **on** OpenJammer". Use the word, not the logo, and don't
  imply endorsement.
- **Unmodified redistribution** — redistribute official, unmodified builds under their name.
- **Talk about the project** — articles, tutorials, videos, course material.

## What requires renaming (a modified fork must rebrand)

- A **modified** version of the kernel or app (a fork) must ship under a **different name and logo** and
  must not present itself as "OpenJammer". (The AGPL guarantees you *may* fork the code; this policy
  ensures a fork doesn't trade on the Marks or confuse users — the protection copyleft alone could not
  provide, as VCV Rack learned.)
- Using the Marks as (part of) your product name, company name, domain, or app-store listing in a way
  that suggests official status or endorsement.
- Using the logo in modified form, or in a way that implies the steward produced or endorses your build,
  plugin, distro, or service.

## Plugins, distros, and the marketplace

- A **plugin/distro** may say it is "for OpenJammer" (nominative use) and may carry any code license,
  including commercial. It may **not** be named "OpenJammer <X>" or use the logo as if first-party.
- **First-party / verified** marketplace status (and any use of the Marks it confers) is granted only
  through the marketplace Terms of Service, never implied.

## Why this matters

The Plugin Exception is what makes a paid ecosystem legal; it intentionally does **not** copyleft
commercial plugins. So the thing that stops a closed competitor from shipping "OpenJammer Pro" and
trading on the community's reputation is the **brand**. Registering and consistently enforcing the Marks
— while keeping nominative use generously open — is how the commons stays trustworthy without walling
anything off.
